INVESTMENT TYPES

Bonds

When you buy a bond, you lend money to a government or company.

What you get

Many bonds pay interest and promise to repay principal at maturity. The issuer must be able to meet those promises.

What can go wrong

An issuer may default. Rising market interest rates generally lower existing fixed-rate bond prices. Inflation can reduce what your payments can buy.

A simple example

A $1,000 bond with a 4% annual coupon pays $40 a year if the issuer pays as promised. Selling before maturity may return more or less than $1,000. Coupon rate is not always the same as your actual yield.

Types of bond risk

U.S. Treasury securities and a financially weak company’s high-yield bonds have very different credit risks. Bond funds can diversify across issuers, but their prices can still fall; most do not promise to return your original investment on a specific maturity date.

For college expenses next year, a long-term bond fund can still fluctuate too much. Match the investment’s risks and access rules to the date you need the money.
