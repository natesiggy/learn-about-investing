INVESTMENT TYPES

Mutual Funds and ETFs

Funds let you invest in a group of stocks, bonds, or other assets.

Mutual funds

A mutual fund combines money from many investors to buy stocks, bonds, or other investments. You buy or sell shares at the fund’s next calculated price, usually set at the end of the trading day. Some mutual funds have managers who choose the investments, while others follow an index.

ETFs

An exchange-traded fund, or ETF, also holds a group of investments, but its shares trade on an exchange throughout the day. Its price changes while the market is open. ETFs can follow an index or use an actively managed strategy.

Index funds

An index fund tries to follow a market index, such as the S&P 500. It can be a mutual fund or an ETF. “Index fund” describes how investments are chosen, while “mutual fund” and “ETF” describe the fund’s structure. A broad stock index fund lets you own parts of many companies through one investment.

Diversification

A broad fund can spread exposure across many companies. A narrow sector fund may still be concentrated. Owning multiple funds with the same holdings may add less diversification than you think. Funds can lose money.

The expense ratio

This is an annual operating cost expressed as a percentage of assets. At a constant $1,000 balance, 0.10% is roughly $1 per year; 1% is roughly $10. Actual fees depend on changing balances and are reflected in fund value. Use the compound lab to see the long-term effect.
