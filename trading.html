INVESTING BASICS

Investing and Trading

Compare holding investments for years with buying and selling over shorter periods.

Long-term investing

Focus on goals, diversification, contributions, costs, and patience. A long holding period still does not remove the possibility of loss.

Frequent trading

Frequent trading means buying and selling often to try to profit from price changes. You have to decide when to get in and when to sell, which can be hard when prices move quickly. Trading costs and taxes can take a share of any gains, and using borrowed money can make losses much larger.

A stock getting attention online

A stock jumps 20%, and posts online keep saying its price will rise further. You have $300 set aside for a concert, with tickets going on sale next month. What do you do?

Put all $300 into it

You expose money with a near-term purpose to a large possible loss. A recent price jump doesn’t establish future returns.

Wait and look into the company before buying

Keep the $300 for your concert tickets. Before deciding whether to buy the stock with other money, look at how the company is doing and why its price has risen. Online attention alone does not tell you whether it is worth buying.

Buy it with a stop-loss so you cannot lose much

A stop order is not a guarantee of the sale price. A price gap or fast market can cause execution below the stop level.

Setting up a paper trading account

Paper trading lets you buy and sell investments with fake money while following market prices. You can try different stocks, learn how orders work, and see what happens to your portfolio without putting real money into those trades.

For example, on TradingView you can sign in, open Supercharts, and open the Trading Panel or Trade menu. Select Paper Trading and choose Connect. Make sure Paper Trading is the selected account before placing an order. Then choose a stock, enter the amount you want to buy, and follow the position in your paper account. You do not need to fund a real brokerage account to use the simulator.

Use a virtual balance close to an amount you might actually invest. Buying huge positions with an unrealistic balance makes it harder to judge whether a strategy would work for you. Paper trading also feels different from risking your own money, so focus on learning how decisions work rather than just chasing the biggest return.

How paper trading helped me

Before I set up a paper trading account, I only had my real investment money in an S&P 500 fund. Paper trading let me try a wider variety of individual stocks without risking my own money. It helped me understand stock trading better because I could see how the stocks I picked moved and how my decisions affected the account.

I was concerned about market swings around the midterm elections and the possibility of an AI bubble. Using fake money let me explore those stocks without taking the same risks with my real savings.
